\section{Transistors}
A transistor is a fundamental semiconductor device that finds its use in
amplification and switching applications. The most widely used transistors
are Bipolar Junction Transistors (BJT) and Field Effect Transistors (FET).
All transistors are three terminal devices. The terminals are named
differently for BJTs and FETs but play similar roles.

\begin{table}[ht]
  \centering
    \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{|c|X|X|X|} \hline
      Type & Terminal 1 & Terminal 2 & Terminal 3 \\ \hline
      BJT & Emitter & Base & Collector \\ \hline
      FET & Source & Gate & Drain \\ \hline
    \end{tabularx}
  \caption{Transistor Terminals}
\end{table}

The operation of a transistor involves controlling the current flow between
two terminals (Collector-Emitter for BJT \& Drain-Source for FET) using
a third terminal (Base for BJT \& Gate for FET). The control mechanism
differs between BJTs and FETs.

\begin{center}
  \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{|c|X|X|X|} \hline
    Type & What Controlling & What Controlled & How To \\ \hline
    BJT & Base & Collector--Emitter & Set Current At Base \\ \hline
    FET & Gate & Drain-Source & Set Voltage At Gate \\ \hline
  \end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\subsection{Modes of Operation}
All transistors use three modes of operation: Cut-Off, Active and
Saturation. They can be summarized as follows:

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
    \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{|c|X|X|X|} \hline
      Mode & State & CE/DS Resistance & Application \\ \hline
      Cut-Off & Off & High & Open-Switch \\ \hline
      Active & Partially On & Moderate & Amplification \\ \hline
      Saturation & Fully On & Low & Closed-Switch \\ \hline
    \end{tabularx}
  \caption{Modes of Transistor Operation}
\end{table}

Unfortunately! Due to historical flaws in FET terminology, many texts often
refer the Active Mode as the Saturation Mode and the Saturation Mode as
Ohmic/Linear/Triode Mode. \textbf{This can be very confusing at first...
Please be careful!}

I've intentionally not used the terms N-Channel or P-Channel classifications
for both BJTs and FETs and Depletion or Enhancement configurations for FETs
as the mode of operation remains the same regardless of these variations.

\subsection{Governing Equations}

Assuming an N-Channel BJT \& an N-Channel Enhancement FET, the conditions for
each mode of operation are as follows:

\begin{table}[ht]
  \centering
    \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{|c|M|M|} \hline
      Mode & $BJT$ & $FET$\\ \hline
      Cut-Off & V_{BE} < V_{th} & V_{GS} < V_{th} \\ \hline
      Active & V_{BE} \geq V_{th} \land V_{CE} > V_{CE(sat)} & V_{GS} \geq V_{th} \land V_{DS} \geq (V_{GS} - V_{th}) \\ \hline
      Saturation & V_{BE} \geq V_{th} \land V_{CE} \leq V_{CE(sat)} & V_{GS} \geq V_{th} \land V_{DS} < (V_{GS} - V_{th}) \\ \hline
    \end{tabularx}
  \caption{Transistor Operating Mode Conditions}
\end{table}

The Eber-Moll model for BJTs and the Shichman-Hodges model for FETs provide
a more detailed representation of transistor behavior. The terminal equations
for each mode of operation are as follows:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Cut-Off Mode:}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item BJT $(V_{BE} < V_{th}): I_B \approx 0\spc I_C \approx 0\spc I_E \approx 0$
      \item FET $(V_{GS} < V_{th}): I_G \approx 0\spc I_D \approx 0\spc I_S \approx 0$
    \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Active Mode:}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item BJT $(V_{BE} \geq V_{th} \land V_{CE} > V_{CE(sat)}): I_C = I_S (e^{V_{BE}/V_T} - 1)\spc I_C = \beta I_B\spc I_E = I_B + I_C$
      \item FET $(V_{GS} \geq V_{th} \land V_{DS} \geq V_{GS}-V_{th}): I_D = k (V_{GS} - V_{th})^2\spc I_G \approx 0\spc I_S = I_D$
    \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Saturation Mode:}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item BJT $(V_{BE} \geq V_{th} \land V_{CE} \leq V_{CE(sat)}): I_C \approx I_{C(sat)}\spc I_E = I_B + I_C$
      \item FET $(V_{GS} \geq V_{th} \land V_{DS} < V_{GS}-V_{th}): I_D = k[(V_{GS}-V_{th})V_{DS} - V_{DS}^2/2]\spc I_G \approx 0\spc I_S = I_D$
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

These are just approximations and the real world current would show slight
deviations due to various secondary effects (i.e. non-idealities).

\subsection{Physical Structure}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{BJT:} Consists of three layers of semiconductor material
    forming two P-N junctions. The layers are named Emitter, Base and
    Collector. The Emitter is heavily doped to inject charge carriers
    efficiently into the Base, which is very thin and lightly doped.
    The Collector is moderately doped to collect carriers from the Base.
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Modelled by coupled P-N diode equations also known as the
        Eber-Moll model. In Active Mode, It reduces to $I_C = I_S
        (e^{V_{BE}/V_T} - 1)$ and $I_E = I_B + I_C$ because the Base-Collector
        junction is reverse biased and can be neglected.
    \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{FET:} Consists of a channel of semiconductor material
    (N-type for N-Channel FETs) with terminals at either end called
    Source and Drain. A Gate terminal is placed above the channel,
    separated by a thin insulating layer (oxide). The Gate voltage
    controls the conductivity of the channel, allowing current to flow
    between Source and Drain when a sufficient voltage is applied.
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Modelled by the Shichman-Hodges equations. In Active Mode,
        It reduces to $I_D = k (V_{GS} - V_{th})^2$ because the channel
        is pinched off near the Drain and the current saturates.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
