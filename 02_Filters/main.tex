\chapter{Filters}

\section{Overview}
This chapter covers various types of electronic filters,
which are circuits designed to selectively allow or block
certain frequency components of a signal. A filter can
be classified either by the type of frequency response it
exhibits (low-pass, high-pass, band-pass, band-stop) or
by the order of the filter (single-pole, double-pole, etc.).
Filters are widely used.

Ideally a filter should have perfect frequency selectivity
that is, (\(-\infty dB\) roll-off in the stop band and \(0 dB\)
attenuation in the pass band), no signal distortion and a
linear phase response. However, real filters deviate from
the ideal due to various non-ideal effects, including finite
roll-off rate, pass band ripple, limited stop band
attenuation, non-linear phase response, component tolerances,
component parasitics, loading effects and temperature
variations.

A filter can be used to remove unwanted frequency components
from a signal such as noise or interference, or to extract
desired frequency components from a signal for further
processing.

\section{Single Pole Filters}
A single pole (first order) filter has a transfer function with
one pole in the s-plane. The decay or rise rate is \(20 dB/\text{decade}\)
or \(6 dB/\text{octave}\).

\section{Double Pole Filters}
A double pole (second order) filter has a transfer function with
two poles in the s-plane. The decay or rise rate is \(40 dB/\text{decade}\)
or \(12 dB/\text{octave}\).
