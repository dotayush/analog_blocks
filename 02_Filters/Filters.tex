\chapter{Filters}

\section{Overview}
This chapter covers various types of electronic filters,
which are circuits designed to selectively allow or block
certain frequency components of a signal.

\section{Behavior}
\paragraph{Ideal:}
An ideal filter has the following characteristics:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Perfect Frequency Selectivity:} Depending
    on the type of filter (low-pass, high-pass, band-pass,
    band-stop), it completely allows or blocks specific
    frequency ranges with 90-degree sharp cutoffs.
  \item \textbf{No Signal Distortion:} The filter does not
    introduce any distortion to the signal within the
    passband.
  \item \textbf{Linear Phase Response:} The phase response
    is linear within the passband, ensuring that all
    frequency components are delayed by the same amount.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Non-Ideal:}
The characteristics of real filters deviate from
the ideal due to various effects, including:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Finite Roll-Off Rate:} Real filters have
    a gradual transition between the passband and stopband,
    characterized by a finite roll-off rate (e.g., 20 dB/decade
    for a first-order filter).
  \item \textbf{Passband Ripple:} Some filters exhibit
    variations in amplitude within the passband, known
    as ripple.
  \item \textbf{Stopband Attenuation:} The filter does not
    completely block signals in the stopband, resulting
    in finite attenuation.
  \item \textbf{Non-Linear Phase Response:} Real filters
    often have a non-linear phase response, leading to
    phase distortion of the signal.
  \item \textbf{Component Tolerances:} Refer to
    \ref{sec:theory_general_tolerances}.
  \item \textbf{Component Parasitics:} Refer to
    \ref{sec:theory_general_parasitics}.
  \item \textbf{Loading Effects:} Refer to
    \ref{sec:theory_general_loading}.
  \item \textbf{Temperature Variations:} Changes in
    temperature can affect the characteristics of
    components, leading to variations in filter
    performance.
\end{itemize}


\section{Usages}
The primary purpose of filters is to manipulate
the frequency content of signals for various applications,
including:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Signal Conditioning:} Filters are used
    to remove unwanted noise or interference from signals,
    improving signal quality for further processing.
  \item \textbf{Audio Processing:} Filters are used
    in audio systems to shape the frequency response,
    such as bass and treble adjustments.
  \item \textbf{Communication Systems:} Filters are
    used to separate different frequency channels,
    enabling multiplexing and demultiplexing of signals.
  \item \textbf{Data Acquisition:} Filters are used
    to prevent aliasing by removing high-frequency
    components before analog-to-digital conversion.
  \item \textbf{Control Systems:} Filters are used
    to smooth sensor signals and reduce noise in feedback
    loops.
\end{itemize}
