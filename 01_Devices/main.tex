\chapter{Devices \& Analysis}

A device is a physical component that can be used to build circuits.
Devices can be classified based on their functionality, characteristics,
and behavior. Here we will classify devices based on if they require
an external power source to operate or not, which leads us to two
categories of devices:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Passive Devices}: These devices do not require a power
      source to operate. They can attenuate, store and dissipate
      electromagnetic energy.
    \item \textbf{Active Devices}: These devices require a power source to
      operate. They can attenuate, store, dissipate \textbf{and amplify}
      electromagnetic energy.
\end{itemize}

There are several important terms we will use throughout this handbook
and it is good to be familiar with them early on.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Port/Terminal}: A point of connection for a device.
      It can be a pin, contact, or lead. Two Terminal and Two Port
      devices are the most common.
    \item \textbf{Linear}: The relationship between two quantities (here
      voltage and current) is proportional by some factor. If you plot
      this relationship, you will get a straight line. If the relationship
      is not proportional, it is non-linear.
    \item \textbf{Time Invariant}: If a quantity does not change with time,
      it is time invariant. Many devices suffer from aging and therefore
      are time variant over long periods. For everyday circuits, we can
      assume that devices are time invariant.
    \item \textbf{Dissipation}: The act of releasing energy (usually in the
      form of heat) is known as dissipation.
\end{enumerate}
