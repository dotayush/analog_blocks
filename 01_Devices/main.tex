\chapter{Devices \& Basic Blocks}

A device is a physical component that can be used to build circuits.
Devices can be classified based on their functionality, characteristics,
and behavior. In the context of analog circuits, devices are
primarily classified into two categories: passive and active devices.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Passive Devices: These do not require an external power
      source to operate. They can only attenuate, store or dissipate
      energy.
    \item Active Devices: These devices require an external power
      source to operate. Alongside attenuating, storing, or dissipating
      energy, they can also amplify signals and provide gain to the
      circuit.
\end{itemize}

We'll often use the term \textbf{one-port}, \textbf{two-port}, or
\textbf{three-port} to describe the number of terminals (pins/contacts)
a device has. We'll also use terms like \textbf{linear} (the relationship
between voltage and current is proportional by a factor), \textbf{time-invariant}
(the relationship between voltage and current does not drift with
time), \textbf{dissipative} (the device dissipates energy usually in the
form of heat).
