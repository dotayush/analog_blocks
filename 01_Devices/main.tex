\chapter{Devices \& Basic Blocks}

A device is a physical component that can be used to build circuits.
Devices can be classified based on their functionality, characteristics,
and behavior. Here we will classify devices based on if they require
an external power source to operate or not. This classification leads
us to two categories of devices.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Passive Devices}: Do not require an external power source to
      operate. They can attenuate, store and dissipate electromagnetic
      energy.
    \item \textbf{Active Devices}: Require an external power source to operate.
      They can attenuate, store and dissipate electromagnetic energy
      but they can also amplify energy.
\end{itemize}

There are several important terms we will use throughout this handbook
and it is good to be familiar with them early on.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Terminal}: A point of connection for a device. It can be
      a pin, contact, or lead.
    \item \textbf{Port}: A pair of terminals that can be used to connect
      a device to other devices or circuits. A port can be used to
      measure voltage and current.
    \item \textbf{Linear}: The relationship between two quantities
      (usually voltage and current) is proportional by a factor.
      Non-linear devices do not have a proportional relationship
      between voltage and current.
    \item \textbf{Time-invariant}: The relationship between two quantities
      (usually voltage and current) does not drift with time. Time-variant
      devices have a relationship between voltage and current that changes
      with time.
    \item \textbf{Dissipative}: The device dissipates energy usually in
      the form of heat. Non-dissipative devices do not dissipate energy.
\end{enumerate}
